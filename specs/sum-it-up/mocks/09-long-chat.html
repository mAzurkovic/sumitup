Claude Sonnet 5.5 ▾
  • Plants, algae, and some bacteria turn light into chemical energy.
  • Sugars are built from carbon dioxide and water; oxygen is released.
  • Light reactions make ATP and NADPH; the Calvin cycle uses them to fix carbon.
  • Supplies most energy for life on Earth and sustains atmospheric oxygen.
What does the Calvin cycle produce?
It produces sugars, using ATP and NADPH from the light reactions.
Where does it happen?
The page says most photosynthesis happens in chloroplasts.
Why does low light slow it down?
Low light slows the light reactions, so the Calvin cycle runs short of ATP and NADPH.
What limits the overall rate?
Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, and temperature. The factor in shortest supply controls the speed.
Why does this matter for farming?
Crop yields depend on how efficiently plants carry out this process.
Which pigment absorbs the light?
Chlorophyll, which absorbs red and blue light.
